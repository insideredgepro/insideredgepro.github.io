Methodology & Data Sources

Last updated: November 2026

This page explains exactly where InsiderEdgePro's data comes from, how it is filtered, and how metrics like cluster buys and performance returns are calculated — so you can judge the data for yourself.

Data sources

  • US insider trades: SEC EDGAR Form 4 filings. Corporate insiders (officers, directors, and 10%+ shareholders) must file Form 4 within two business days of a trade. We track open-market purchases (transaction code "P").
  • Congressional trades: disclosures filed under the STOCK Act of 2012, which requires members of Congress to report trades within 45 days. Filings are sourced from the official House and Senate public disclosure portals.
  • Canadian insider trades: SEDI (System for Electronic Disclosure by Insiders), Canada's mandatory insider reporting system covering TSX and TSX-V issuers.

Update frequency

US and Canadian data is refreshed approximately every 30 minutes during market hours. Congressional disclosures are surfaced shortly after they appear in the public record. Each dashboard shows a "last updated" timestamp.

Filtering thresholds

  • US dashboard: open-market purchases of $25,000 or more.
  • Canada dashboard: purchases of CAD$15,000 or more.

Smaller trades are excluded deliberately. Large, voluntary, open-market purchases by executives are a higher-conviction signal than routine small transactions, option exercises, or grants.

Cluster buy detection

A cluster buy is flagged when two or more distinct insiders at the same company file open-market purchases within a short rolling window. Academic research on insider trading has found multi-insider buying to be a stronger signal than single-insider purchases. Cluster detection runs across all three markets we cover.

Performance tracking

The performance views measure the simple price return from each insider's disclosed purchase price (or filing-date price where noted) to the latest available closing price. Price snapshots are refreshed hourly on weekdays for US listings and daily for TSX/TSX-V listings. Returns do not account for dividends, splits beyond price adjustment, or transaction costs, and past performance does not indicate future results.

Limitations

  • Form 4 data reflects what insiders report; occasional filing errors or amendments originate at the source.
  • Congressional disclosure amounts are reported in ranges (e.g. $15K–$50K), not exact figures.
  • STOCK Act filings can lag the trade date by up to 45 days by law.
  • We display buys prominently because open-market purchases carry the clearest signal; sales can happen for many non-informational reasons (diversification, taxes, planned sales).
